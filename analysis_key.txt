MAIN BUSINESS QUESTION:
Why are customers churning, and what can ShopPulse do to reduce churn?

2. Missing Orders
Customer C016 has a missing Orders value.

3. Negative Orders

These should be treated as suspicious/invalid values.

5. Duplicate Customer

CHURN PATTERNS

A useful observation is that many churned customers have high Last_Login_Days.

Examples:

C005 - Churn: Yes - Last Login: 40 days
C007 - Churn: Yes - Last Login: 60 days
C009 - Churn: Yes - Last Login: 32 days
C014 - Churn: Yes - Last Login: 45 days
C017 - Churn: Yes - Last Login: 52 days
C019 - Churn: Yes - Last Login: 35 days
C023 - Churn: Yes - Last Login: 48 days
C026 - Churn: Yes - Last Login: 55 days
C028 - Churn: Yes - Last Login: 29 days
C030 - Churn: Yes - Last Login: 38 days

This suggests that customer inactivity may be associated with churn.

IMPORTANT:
Association does NOT prove causation.

A good analyst should say:
"Customers with longer periods since their last login appear more likely to churn, but this dataset alone does not prove that inactivity causes churn."

OTHER USEFUL OBSERVATIONS

Churned customers often have:

- Lower order activity
- Lower total spending
- Longer periods since last login

However, there are exceptions.

Therefore, the student should avoid making absolute claims.

RECOMMENDED ANALYSIS ORDER

1. Check data quality.
2. Remove or flag invalid values.
3. Standardize customer types.
4. Remove duplicate C050.
5. Compare churned and non-churned customers.
6. Examine Last_Login_Days.
7. Examine Orders and Total_Amount.
8. Form a hypothesis.
9. Consider limitations.
10. Give a business recommendation.

EXPECTED BUSINESS RECOMMENDATION

ShopPulse could introduce an early-warning system for inactive customers.

For example:

- Identify customers who have not logged in for a long period.
- Send personalized re-engagement messages.
- Offer targeted promotions where appropriate.
- Monitor whether these interventions reduce churn.

LIMITATIONS

The dataset is small and contains intentionally messy data.

The analysis shows patterns but cannot prove causation.

Additional information such as customer complaints, product usage, subscription history and reasons for cancellation would improve the analysis.

GOOD FINAL ANSWER

"Customer inactivity appears to be strongly associated with churn in this dataset. I would first clean the data, standardize customer categories and remove duplicates. Then I would identify inactive customers as an early-warning group and test targeted re-engagement strategies. However, I would not claim that inactivity directly causes churn without additional evidence."
